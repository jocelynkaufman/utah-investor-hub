The Salt Lake County Market

Real Estate Investing in Salt Lake County

Salt Lake County is Utah’s largest and most urbanized county — home to the state capital, the airport, the University of Utah, the biggest hospital systems, and most of the state’s private-sector jobs. For investors, that density is the whole point. More tenants, more employers, more varied housing stock, and more entry paths than anywhere else in Utah.

From Avenues bungalows and Rose Park duplexes to Sandy family rentals, Draper townhomes, and brand-new SFH stock in Herriman and South Jordan, Salt Lake County gives you a different strategy menu depending on which corridor you point your map at. Appreciation plays live on the east bench. Cash-flow plays live out in the southwest valley. BRRRRs still work in the pockets where older stock hasn’t been renovated yet.

The Character of the County

What Makes Salt Lake County Work for Investors

Salt Lake County holds more than a third of Utah’s population in a valley hemmed in by the Wasatch Range on the east and the Oquirrhs on the west. That geography is the whole investing thesis. Land is scarce, sprawl is physically bounded, and demand keeps pushing into every remaining buildable pocket. Over the past decade the county has absorbed waves of in-migration from California, Texas, and the Pacific Northwest — and Utah’s own household-formation numbers remain some of the strongest in the country. Tenants don’t need to be convinced to live here. They’re already looking.

The employer base is unusually diversified for a Mountain West metro. Tech, healthcare, financial services, government, higher ed, logistics, and construction all show up at scale. Salt Lake City proper anchors the knowledge-worker jobs — downtown offices, the U of U medical campus, the research corridor along Foothill Boulevard. The south-valley suburbs catch the sprawl of Silicon Slopes commuters who work in Lehi but want more square footage per dollar. And the west side of the valley carries most of the warehousing, logistics, and manufacturing economy that keeps the blue-collar rental pool deep.

What this means for investors is that you’re never running a one-strategy bet in Salt Lake County. House hacking works in the older neighborhoods of Salt Lake City proper. Buy-and-hold family rentals work in Sandy, South Jordan, and Herriman. BRRRR math still pencils in Magna, Taylorsville, and parts of West Valley where the renovated-comp gap hasn’t closed yet. Mid-term and corporate rentals work near the medical campuses. Short-term rentals exist but are tightly licensed and small as a share of the county’s overall rental stock.

Zoom In

Cities in Salt Lake County We Cover

Seven submarkets, seven different rental stories. The right city depends on your strategy, budget, and how hands-on you want to be.

The Capital Market

Salt Lake City

Downtown offices, the University of Utah medical campus, and the deepest tenant pool in the state. Classic house-hack territory in older neighborhoods like the Avenues, Capitol Hill, Sugar House, and 9th & 9th — plus BRRRR opportunity on the westside corridor through Rose Park, Glendale, and Poplar Grove.

Deep dive into Salt Lake City

South Valley Family Suburb

Sandy

A mature south-valley suburb with established schools, mountain access out to Little Cottonwood, and a stable family-rental demand profile. Older 70s/80s SFH stock plus newer townhome infill. Tenants tend to be long-tenure families, which keeps turnover low and management quiet.

Tech-Adjacent & Point of the Mountain

Draper

Sits at the south end of Salt Lake County, right against the Point of the Mountain and the Silicon Slopes commute into Lehi. Newer single-family and townhome stock, higher-income tenants, and a buyer pool with a strong appreciation bias. Family rentals and corporate housing both work here.

Deep dive into Draper

The Daybreak Corridor

South Jordan

Home to Daybreak — one of the largest master-planned communities in the West — plus a growing tech and healthcare employment base along Bangerter Highway. Newer SFH and townhome stock, HOA-heavy, strong demand from relocating families who want new construction without the Lehi price tag.

Southwest Valley Growth

Herriman

The county’s southwest growth frontier. Newer SFH neighborhoods, young families, Mountain View Corridor access. Best fit for straightforward buy-and-hold single-family rentals where you’re leaning on appreciation plus modest cash flow rather than value-add math.

Affordable West-Side Entry

Taylorsville

Just west of I-215, Taylorsville is one of the lowest-entry SLC-metro markets. Older 1960s–80s ramblers and split-levels fill the housing stock — strong BRRRR territory and a good fit for basement-ADU conversions. Salt Lake Community College’s Redwood Campus and the I-215 commute both feed steady long-term tenant demand.

Deep dive into Taylorsville

Utah’s Second-Largest City

West Valley City

Utah’s second-largest city by population, with the deepest existing multi-family stock in the state outside Salt Lake proper. 1970s–80s SFH along the 3500 S and 4100 S corridors, duplex/triplex inventory in the Granger area, and newer townhomes near the West Valley Center TRAX stop. Affordable entry prices and high transactional volume.

Best Investing Strategies in Salt Lake County

Four strategies that fit the biggest Utah county — chosen for the stock, the tenant base, and the submarkets where deals still pencil.

Live-In Entry Point

House Hacking in Salt Lake County

Salt Lake City proper is one of Utah’s best house-hack markets — older homes with basement ADUs, duplex layouts tucked into mixed-use streets, and strong rental demand within a 10-minute drive of downtown. Owner-occupant financing (FHA, conventional 5% down) makes the entry math work.

Learn the strategy

Recycle Your Capital

BRRRR in Salt Lake County

The westside of the valley — Magna, parts of West Valley, Taylorsville, Kearns — still has older homes that trade below their renovated comps. That spread is where BRRRR math lives. Buy distressed, rehab, rent, refinance against new appraised value, and redeploy most of your capital into the next deal.

Learn the strategy

Steady Cash Flow

Long-Term Rentals in the South Valley

Sandy, South Jordan, and Herriman are the family-rental core of the county. 12-month leases, long-tenure tenants, and a buyer pool that keeps appreciation steady year over year. Lower cap rates than the westside, but lower turnover and easier management.

Learn the strategy

Multiple Units, One Closing

Multi-Family in Salt Lake County

Salt Lake City proper has the deepest small-multifamily stock in the state — duplexes, triplexes, and fourplexes that pre-date the single-family zoning wave. Two-to-four unit buildings still qualify for residential financing. Five-plus properties move into commercial loan territory with bigger upside.
