The Utah Edge

Why Invest in Utah Real Estate

Fast-growing population, tech-driven job market, investor-friendly laws, and financing paths that still work for first-time buyers.

📈

Population & Rental Demand

Utah has been one of the fastest-growing states for over a decade, with the population climbing roughly 1.4% per year. Growth drives rental demand along the Wasatch Front — Salt Lake County, Utah County, Davis County, and Weber County all have tight vacancy and steady rent growth.

💻

Silicon Slopes Tech Economy

The tech corridor between Salt Lake and Provo has drawn Adobe, Qualtrics, Ancestry, Pluralsight, and hundreds of startups. High-paying tech jobs mean stronger tenant incomes, lower vacancy, and consistent demand for both long-term and short-term rentals.

⚖️

Investor-Friendly Laws

Utah’s landlord-tenant law is reasonably investor-friendly: 3-day pay-or-quit notices, a straightforward eviction process, no statewide rent control. Combined with no state-level STR ban, it’s an easier state to operate in than California, Washington, or Oregon.

🏘

Owner-Occupant Financing

FHA, conventional 5%-down, VA, and Utah Housing Corporation loan programs make the first investment property genuinely accessible. House hacking a duplex or fourplex with 3.5% down is a real path in Utah — and the stock is still here in Ogden, west valley, and older SLC neighborhoods.

Common Questions

Utah Investor FAQ

The questions Utah investors ask us most — answered straight.

What is house hacking in Utah?

House hacking means living in part of a property — one unit of a duplex, a basement apartment, or a spare bedroom — while renting out the rest. In Utah, it qualifies for owner-occupant financing (3.5% down with FHA, 5% down with conventional), making it the most capital-efficient way to get started. Read the house hacking guide →

Where should I invest in Utah real estate?

The Wasatch Front — Salt Lake County, Utah County, Davis County, and Weber County — has the best combination of rental demand, population growth, and financing availability. Ogden, Clearfield, West Valley, and parts of Salt Lake City proper still have inventory where investor numbers work.

How much money do I need to start investing in Utah?

For owner-occupant strategies like house hacking, you can start with as little as 3.5%–5% of the purchase price. For pure investment properties you don’t live in, expect 20–25% down plus reserves. The Utah Housing Corporation (UHC) offers down payment assistance for first-time buyers that can reduce the out-of-pocket cost further.

Can I invest in Utah real estate from out of state?

Yes — many Utah investors live out of state. The key is building the right team: an investor-friendly Utah agent, a property manager, and a Utah-based lender. Jocelyn’s Stodd Group team handles everything from analysis to closing for out-of-state investors, including FaceTime walkthroughs and video tours.

What markets in Utah are best for short-term rentals?

Park City and Deer Valley (ski), Moab and the Zion/Bryce area (national parks), and St. George (winter escape and golf) are Utah’s top short-term rental markets. Each city regulates STRs separately — Park City licensing is strict and Moab tightens rules every year. Always confirm current zoning before you offer. Read the STR guide →

What financing options exist for Utah investors?

Owner-occupant loans (FHA, VA, Conventional 5% down, UHC programs) for house hacks and first homes; investment-property loans (20–25% down) for pure rentals; hard money or private lending for BRRRR and flips; commercial loans for 5+ unit multi-family.
