The Ogden Market

Real Estate Investing in Ogden

Ogden is where the Wasatch Front’s value math still makes sense. It’s the northernmost major city on the FrontRunner line, it has a university, a growing tech employer base, and the kind of pre-war housing stock that almost doesn’t exist in newer Utah suburbs. For an investor priced out of Salt Lake, Ogden is often the first serious alternative.

The city has quietly reinvented itself over the past decade. Historic 25th Street is now a walkable restaurant and shopping district. The Jefferson neighborhood is in the middle of a long gentrification cycle. Weber State keeps churning out a steady rental pool. Prices are still well below Salt Lake County, which is why BRRRR and first house hacks keep landing here.

The Character of the Market

What Makes Ogden Unique for Investors

The first thing to know about Ogden: the housing stock is old. Much of the city was built during the railroad era, and whole neighborhoods still carry 1900–1940 bungalows, Victorians, and small duplexes that were originally purpose-built for shift workers. For a disciplined investor, that’s a feature. Older stock means wider spreads between distressed pricing and renovated comp value — which is exactly the gap BRRRR investors need.

The second thing is the revitalization story. Downtown Ogden around 25th Street has been pulling in restaurants, a brewery scene, and small offices for years. The Jefferson District just east of downtown has been quietly gentrifying, with the kind of block-by-block appreciation that rewards early buyers. Neither of these shifts is finished. Further east toward Weber State and the mountains, prices are already higher, but the long-term appreciation story has been strong.

The third is the commuter story. FrontRunner’s Ogden station connects straight to downtown Salt Lake, and Hill Air Force Base sits just south of the city. Both anchor a layer of tenants who work in the broader Wasatch economy but want to live where their mortgage or rent is lower. That’s a resilient tenant profile: salary-paid, multi-year commitments, not dependent on a single employer.

Best Strategies for Ogden

Four strategies that fit Ogden’s stock and tenant base — chosen because they still pencil at Ogden entry prices.

The Ogden Signature Play

BRRRR in Ogden

Ogden was built for BRRRR. Older, deferred-maintenance homes in neighborhoods with a clear improvement curve mean the buy-rehab-rent-refinance cycle can actually recycle a meaningful share of your capital. Jefferson, East Central, and the blocks around 25th Street are where investors tend to look first.

First Deal Entry Point

House Hacking in Ogden

Lower entry prices mean a first-time Utah investor can realistically buy a duplex or a SFH with ADU here without stretching the budget. Owner-occupant financing plus Ogden rent comps often turn into the kind of near-break-even housing cost that house hacking is supposed to deliver.

Steady Cash Flow

Long-Term Rentals in Ogden

Because Ogden entry prices are lower than Salt Lake’s but rents are closer than you’d expect, the pure long-term rental math often clears here on properties that wouldn’t pencil further south. Weber State students, Hill Air Force Base personnel, and FrontRunner commuters all feed the tenant pool.

Multiple Units, One Closing

Small Multi-Family in Ogden

Ogden’s older grid has plenty of true small-multifamily buildings — 2–4 unit converted Victorians, purpose-built fourplexes, and side-by-side duplexes. Residential financing still applies, and the per-door price point is some of the most accessible on the Wasatch Front.

What to Buy Here

Rental Property Types That Work in Ogden

Pre-war bungalow with a BRRRR path

Small, often distressed 1920s–1940s bungalows are the Ogden starter BRRRR. Low acquisition cost, clear rehab scope — roof, mechanicals, kitchen, bath — and strong rent-to-value math once renovated.

Converted Victorian duplex

Many of Ogden’s larger historic homes have already been legally split into duplex or triplex configurations. These sit in the sweet spot where small-multifamily financing still applies but you’re buying real character stock.

Near-Weber State student rental

Within a short drive of Weber State’s campus, SFHs rent well as by-the-room student housing. Lower rent per room than BYU-area Provo, but also lower acquisition cost.

Family SFH near FrontRunner

A conventional 3-bed, 2-bath home a short drive from Ogden’s FrontRunner station rents to commuters who want the Salt Lake paycheck without the Salt Lake price tag. Long tenancies, predictable payers.

Townhome on the east bench

For a hands-off investor, newer townhomes on Ogden’s east side offer HOA-covered exteriors and a tenant base of young professionals who still want proximity to the Weber Canyon trails and the resorts above.
