The Utah County Market

Real Estate Investing in Utah County

Utah County is where two of the strongest economic engines in the Mountain West sit back-to-back. BYU anchors the south end around Provo and Orem. Silicon Slopes anchors the north end around Lehi, Draper’s neighbor at the Point of the Mountain. The corridor between them is one of the fastest-growing stretches of Utah, and the rental market reflects that.

Every stage of tenant sits inside this county. Students and young families in Provo. BYU staff and Orem commuters in the middle. Tech workers in Lehi and American Fork earning paychecks that rival any metro in the country. Investors who understand which submarket matches which strategy do very well here.

The Character of the County

What Makes Utah County Work for Investors

Utah County’s investing story is really two stories stacked on top of each other. The southern half, centered on Provo and Orem, is a student-and-family market driven by Brigham Young University and Utah Valley University. The northern half — Lehi, American Fork, Pleasant Grove, Lindon — is the Silicon Slopes tech corridor where Adobe, Qualtrics, Pluralsight, Ancestry, and dozens of smaller tech companies are headquartered. The two halves are twenty minutes apart on I-15 but their rental economics are completely different.

South end: BYU students alone generate thousands of lease signings every year, and the school’s demographics (students leaving on mission, returning missionaries, young married couples, grad students) keep the tenant pool churning in predictable cycles. Provo’s older housing stock near campus still supports classic duplex-and-basement house hacks. Orem bridges student and family rental demand — closer to UVU, a quieter management profile, broader tenant mix.

North end: Silicon Slopes is one of the highest-paying job clusters in Utah. Household incomes in Lehi, Alpine, and Highland run well above the state average, and tenant quality follows. The trade-off is the entry price — Lehi SFH and townhome purchase prices are now among the highest outside of Park City, which means cash-on-cash returns compress and the play tilts toward appreciation plus steady long-term rentals rather than high-yield cash flow. American Fork and Pleasant Grove sit a tier below Lehi on price and still carry strong Silicon Slopes commuter demand, which is where a lot of mid-career investors find their sweet spot.

Cities in Utah County We Cover

Five submarkets along the I-15 spine — from the BYU campus at the south end to the Silicon Slopes tech cluster at the north.

The University Market

Provo

BYU anchors everything south of Center Street. Older housing stock near campus, strong duplex-house-hack inventory, and a predictable student-rental cycle that rewards investors who understand the semester rhythm. Also a growing young-professional market in the downtown core and along the Provo River.

Deep dive into Provo

UVU + Family Rentals

Orem

Bridges Provo student demand and the north-end tech commuter base. Utah Valley University draws a quieter student tenant profile than BYU, and mid-century family homes with basement apartments are the standard house-hack vehicle here. Easier management overhead than Provo proper, slightly lower rent ceilings.

Silicon Slopes HQ

Lehi

The epicenter of Silicon Slopes. Adobe, Qualtrics, Pluralsight, Ancestry, Entrata — and a tenant base of tech workers earning salaries that support premium rents. Newer SFH and townhome stock, higher entry price, lower cap rates, and a strong appreciation profile. Long-term rentals and corporate/mid-term rentals both make sense here.

Deep dive into Lehi

Family-Rental Sweet Spot

American Fork

A tier below Lehi on price but still deeply tied into the Silicon Slopes commute. Broader mix of older and newer SFH stock, strong family tenant demand, and an active downtown revival. One of the most popular starter-investor markets in Utah County for straightforward buy-and-hold single-family rentals.

Growing Family Suburb

Pleasant Grove

North of American Fork, east of Lindon, Pleasant Grove is one of the fastest-growing SFH submarkets in the county. Newer construction, young families, and easy access to both Silicon Slopes jobs and the mountain recreation corridor up American Fork Canyon. Solid long-term rental territory.

Best Investing Strategies in Utah County

Four strategies tuned to the two economies that drive this county — the university market at the south end and Silicon Slopes at the north.

Live-In Entry Point

House Hacking Near BYU

Provo’s older neighborhoods around campus are built for house hacking. Basement apartments are the local norm, duplex zoning still shows up, and student renters sign leases on the semester calendar. Owner-occupant financing plus steady tenant demand is a very forgiving combination for a first deal.

Learn the strategy

Steady Cash Flow

Long-Term Rentals in Silicon Slopes

Lehi, American Fork, and Pleasant Grove run on 12-month leases to tech-worker tenants. Higher entry prices than the south end but lower turnover, stronger tenant credit, and an appreciation story that has compounded aggressively over the last decade.

Learn the strategy

Recycle Your Capital

BRRRR in Provo & Orem

The older housing pockets in Provo and Orem still have homes that trade below renovated comps. Add a legal basement unit, modernize the kitchen and systems, refinance into a 30-year rental loan, and pull your capital back out for the next one. Student-rental demand keeps occupancy strong post-rehab.

Learn the strategy

Multiple Units, One Closing

Multi-Family Around Provo

Small multifamily buildings cluster around BYU and UVU. Fourplexes and triplexes zoned for student housing are a long-standing Provo investor asset — just confirm current city occupancy rules per bedroom before offering. Two-to-four unit still qualifies for residential financing.
