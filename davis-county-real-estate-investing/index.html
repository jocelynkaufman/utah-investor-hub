The Davis County Market

Real Estate Investing in Davis County

Davis County is the family-suburb spine of the Wasatch Front just north of Salt Lake — the stretch of I-15 and the FrontRunner commuter-rail line that runs from Bountiful up through Layton and Clearfield. It’s the market most Utah investors underrate. Entry prices sit meaningfully below Salt Lake County’s south-valley suburbs, the schools are consistently strong, and Hill Air Force Base anchors a tenant pool that doesn’t move with the business cycle.

The thesis here is simple: stable long-term single-family rentals, steady appreciation, and a tenant mix split between Hill AFB personnel, FrontRunner commuters heading into downtown Salt Lake, and workers in the growing retail-and-logistics economy along the I-15 corridor. If you want a Utah rental that runs quietly for a decade, Davis County is where to start looking.

The Character of the County

What Makes Davis County Work for Investors

The economic anchor of Davis County is Hill Air Force Base. Hill is one of the largest single-site employers in Utah and the backbone of the state’s aerospace cluster — airmen, civilian contractors, aerospace engineers, maintenance and depot workers, and the households attached to all of them. That’s a tenant base that rotates on military timelines rather than on the local job market, which gives Davis County rentals a kind of demand floor most Utah submarkets don’t have. When the broader housing market softens, Hill keeps staffing; when it heats up, Hill is still staffing. Every city along the I-15 corridor from Clinton through Clearfield to Layton feels that demand directly.

The second economic driver is commuter access. FrontRunner stations in Woods Cross, Farmington, Layton, and Clearfield put downtown Salt Lake inside a single-seat rail ride, and I-15 keeps the car commute to the capital reasonable outside of peak traffic. That’s why cities like Bountiful, Centerville, Kaysville, and Farmington function as classic family-suburb rentals for Salt Lake professionals who want more square footage than Sugar House or the Avenues delivers. The commuter overlay turns Davis County into a bedroom market for the whole north half of the Wasatch Front job base.

The housing stock reflects both drivers. You’ll find mid-century ramblers and split-levels throughout Layton, Clearfield, Roy-adjacent Clinton and Syracuse, and West Point — the classic working-rental inventory tied to Hill. Farther south in Bountiful and Centerville the stock is older and more established, with mature trees and higher-priced single-family homes. In Farmington and Kaysville, newer master-planned growth has filled in around Station Park and the FrontRunner line. Across all of it, the rental math leans toward straightforward buy-and-hold single-family: modest cash flow, steady appreciation, and very quiet management. It’s not a BRRRR-heavy county the way Weber is, and it’s not an STR market. It’s the long-hold family-rental tier of Utah.

Zoom In

Cities in Davis County We Cover

Nine submarkets strung along I-15 and the FrontRunner line — from Bountiful at the south end up to Clinton and West Point at the Hill AFB corridor.

The Davis Hub

Layton

The largest city in Davis County and the retail and commercial center of the corridor. Hill AFB sits on its west edge, FrontRunner connects it to the rest of the Front, and the housing mix runs from older 1970s and 1980s ramblers to newer townhome and SFH infill. Deep rental pool, strong family-tenant base, and the most consistent appreciation profile in the county.

Deep dive into Layton

Hill AFB Front Gate

Clearfield

Right at the Hill Air Force Base front gate. Classic airman-and-contractor rental market — steady demand, long-tenure military households, and some of the lowest SFH entry prices in Davis County. A FrontRunner station in town and a city core that keeps modernizing make this one of the cleaner starter-investor markets on the Front.

The Established Suburb

Bountiful

The first city north of Salt Lake, close enough to downtown SLC that the commute feels like an inside-Salt-Lake-County trip. Older established neighborhoods, mature east-bench streets, and a rental tenant base that often includes Salt Lake professionals who want more square footage per dollar. Lower turnover, slower rent growth, very stable hold.

Deep dive into Bountiful

Family-Rental Premium

Kaysville

One of the stronger school-district pulls in Davis County, and the price tier reflects it. Established neighborhoods, well-kept SFH stock, and a tenant pool that leans toward long-tenure renters who stay for the established schools. Lower cap rates than Clearfield or Clinton, offset by very quiet management and strong resale demand.

FrontRunner Growth Node

Farmington

Home to Station Park, a major FrontRunner stop, and a decade of newer master-planned growth around Lagoon and the rail corridor. Cleaner new-construction SFH, strong family-rental demand, and a submarket that leans on appreciation and modest cash flow rather than value-add math. A strong fit for investors who want new stock without Lehi pricing.

Quiet South-County Suburb

Centerville

Between Bountiful and Farmington, Centerville is the kind of town investors overlook until they tour it. Established east-bench neighborhoods, easy I-15 and Legacy Parkway access, and a long-tenure tenant base. Appreciation-leaning with the same quiet management profile as Bountiful.

Fast-Growth Westside

Syracuse

On the west side of Davis County out toward Antelope Island. One of the fastest-growing SFH submarkets in the county, with newer family neighborhoods, young households, and easy access to both Hill AFB and the commuter corridor. Leans appreciation-first with steady long-term rental demand.

Newer West Davis

West Point

North of Syracuse and west of Clinton, West Point is primarily newer SFH subdivisions filling in the west-Davis build-out. Hill AFB commuters and a tenant pool that mirrors Syracuse. Newer stock keeps maintenance overhead low early in the hold.

Hill AFB Bedroom Community

Clinton

Tucked between Clearfield and Syracuse, Clinton has become one of the practical Hill AFB bedroom communities. Mid-century ramblers plus newer infill, affordable entry prices compared to Kaysville or Farmington, and a tenant pool that leans military and workforce. A straightforward long-term SFH rental play.

Pick Your Play

Best Investing Strategies in Davis County

Four strategies tuned to a family-suburb rental market — long-term SFH holds, straightforward house hacks, 2–4 unit buildings where they exist, and selective BRRRR in the older pockets.

The Core Davis Play

Long-Term SFH Rentals

Layton, Clearfield, Clinton, Syracuse, and Kaysville are classic SFH long-term rental territory. Hill AFB personnel, FrontRunner commuters, and a stable school-driven tenant base keep leases quiet and turnover low. The most forgiving long-hold environment in the county.

Learn the strategy

Live-In Entry Point

House Hacking in Davis County

Mid-century ramblers and split-levels in Layton, Clearfield, and Clinton routinely come with basement layouts that house-hack cleanly. Owner-occupant financing on a 2–4 unit or an SFH with a legal basement apartment is a practical first-deal path for Hill-adjacent investors or Salt Lake commuters priced out of SLC proper.

Learn the strategy

Recycle Your Capital

Selective BRRRR

The older mid-century pockets of Layton, Clearfield, and Bountiful still carry homes that trade below renovated comps. The BRRRR spread is narrower here than in Ogden, but the exit rents are stronger and the tenant quality is higher — a cleaner play for an investor who’s already done one or two rehabs.

Learn the strategy

Multiple Units, One Closing

Small Multi-Family

Davis County’s small-multifamily inventory is thinner than Ogden’s, but duplexes and the occasional fourplex do show up in Bountiful, Layton, and Clearfield. Two-to-four unit still qualifies for residential financing. A good way to scale per closing while keeping the Hill AFB and FrontRunner tenant pool working for you.
