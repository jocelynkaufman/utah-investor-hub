The Weber County Market

Real Estate Investing in Weber County

Weber County is the value corridor of the Wasatch Front. Ogden anchors it — a historic rail town with pre-WWII bungalows, craftsman homes, and small multifamily stock you simply can’t buy in Salt Lake County at the same price. Weber State University sits above the city on the east bench. Hill Air Force Base sits just across the county line to the south. The BRRRR math works here better than anywhere else on the Front, and the house-hack entry price is some of the most forgiving in Utah.

The thesis is straightforward. Buy older stock below its renovated comp, bring it up to current standards, rent to a tenant pool fed by Weber State, Hill AFB, Ogden’s downtown revival, and the IHC Mckay-Dee medical campus, then refinance against the new appraised value and redeploy. It’s the Utah investor’s classic entry county.

The Character of the County

What Makes Weber County Work for Investors

Ogden was built as a rail town in the late 1800s and early 1900s, and the city’s housing stock still reflects that. Pre-WWII bungalows, craftsman homes, four-squares, and small multifamily buildings are everywhere between the rail yard and Harrison Boulevard. That older stock is the whole reason Weber County penciled for investors through the last decade of Wasatch Front appreciation. You can still find homes that trade well below their renovated east-bench comps, and the gap between those two numbers is where BRRRR math lives.

The demand side is more diverse than Ogden’s value reputation suggests. Weber State University sits above the city on the east bench and feeds a steady student, grad-student, staff, and faculty rental pool. Downtown Ogden’s 25th Street revival has pulled in restaurants, coworking, boutique retail, and a new generation of young-professional renters. Hill Air Force Base sits just across the Davis County line and spills demand into Roy, Riverdale, and South Ogden. The McKay-Dee medical campus anchors a mid-term and traveling-nurse rental niche. Together they keep the tenant pool deep across a wide mix of unit types and price points.

The geography matters too. The Ogden River runs through the city, Weber Canyon opens into the mountains at Ogden’s south edge, and Snowbasin Resort sits a short drive up the canyon. That mountain proximity has been a quiet tailwind on the downtown and east-bench markets. Meanwhile, the north-county cities — North Ogden, Pleasant View, and the Ogden Valley over the mountain — are functionally different submarkets: newer SFH, family rentals, and quieter long-hold math. If you’re an investor whose first deal needs to actually cash-flow, Weber County is the shortest path to that outcome on the Wasatch Front.

Zoom In

Cities in Weber County We Cover

Seven submarkets anchored by Ogden — from the 25th Street core to the North Ogden foothills and the Hill AFB spillover communities to the south.

The Weber County Anchor

Ogden

Historic rail-town stock, the 25th Street downtown revival, Weber State University on the east bench, and the strongest BRRRR math on the Wasatch Front. Older west-side neighborhoods still trade below their renovated comps, and the student, young-professional, and medical tenant pools keep rents strong post-rehab.

Deep dive into Ogden

Family Suburb

North Ogden

Tucked against Ben Lomond Peak at the north end of the county. Newer family neighborhoods, strong schools, and a quieter long-hold environment than Ogden proper. Tenants skew long-tenure families, management stays quiet, and appreciation has been meaningful over the last decade.

Just South of Ogden

South Ogden

A quieter, more residential neighbor to Ogden proper — often a better fit for investors who want Weber-County entry prices without the older rough-around-the-edges west-side stock. Mid-century SFH and ramblers, stable tenant base, and a solid first-rental market.

Hill AFB Spillover

Roy

Sits between Ogden and Clearfield on the Davis County line and functions as a Hill Air Force Base bedroom community. Older ramblers, split-levels, and ranch homes make up most of the stock — and rental demand is dependable year-round. One of the cleaner value-add and buy-and-hold markets on the north Front.

The Retail Corridor

Riverdale

Just south of Ogden along the Riverdale Road retail corridor and I-84. A workforce-rental submarket with proximity to both Hill AFB and the Ogden job base, steady SFH rental demand, and some of the most straightforward long-term rental math in the county.

North Weber Growth

Pleasant View

At the north end of the county next to North Ogden, Pleasant View has grown steadily with newer SFH subdivisions, young families, and the same mountain-adjacent appeal as the rest of north Weber. Appreciation-leaning with a quieter tenant mix than central Ogden.

Older Workforce Stock

Washington Terrace

A small city tucked between South Ogden and Riverdale. Mostly older mid-century SFH and small multi-unit stock, affordable entry prices for the county, and a steady workforce-rental base. A classic quiet buy-and-hold submarket with limited new construction competition.

Pick Your Play

Best Investing Strategies in Weber County

Four strategies tuned to a value market — BRRRR in the older Ogden pockets, house hacking near downtown and Weber State, small multi-family where the stock is deepest, and long-term rentals in the outer cities.

The Flagship Weber Play

BRRRR in Ogden

The strongest BRRRR math on the Wasatch Front lives in Weber County. Older homes in west and central Ogden still trade well below renovated east-bench comps, which keeps the buy-rehab-refinance spread alive. Rent levels post-rehab are supported by Weber State plus Ogden’s steady young-professional pull.

Live-In Entry Point

House Hacking Near Weber State

Ogden’s older housing stock is one of the most forgiving house-hack environments in Utah. Basement apartments are common, duplex and triplex zoning still shows up around downtown and the east-bench Weber State neighborhoods, and the entry price is dramatically lower than Salt Lake proper. First-time investors routinely start here.

Multiple Units, One Closing

Small Multi-Family in Ogden

Ogden has one of the deepest small-multifamily inventories in northern Utah — duplexes, triplexes, and fourplexes from the city’s pre-WWII rail-town era. Residential financing still applies on 2–4 unit buildings. A great way to scale per closing once you’ve done a house hack or two in the county.

Steady Cash Flow

Long-Term Rentals in Roy & South Ogden

The outer Weber cities — Roy, South Ogden, Riverdale, Washington Terrace — are classic SFH long-term rental territory. Hill AFB spillover, Ogden-job commuters, and stable workforce tenants keep leases quiet. A cleaner first-deal environment than central Ogden if you’d rather not take on heavy rehab.
