The Draper Market

Real Estate Investing in Draper

Draper sits at the south end of Salt Lake County, where the Wasatch bends toward Utah County and the Point of the Mountain tech corridor takes over. It’s one of the Salt Lake metro’s most upscale residential cities — Corner Canyon, SunCrest, Suncrest Drive views — with a tenant pool that leans toward tech executives, specialists, and the kind of household that will pay a premium for a newer home with a mountain view.

And then there’s "The Point" — the former State Prison site that’s being redeveloped into a mixed-use community over the next decade. That project will reshape the north end of Draper and probably pull a fresh wave of commercial and residential investment with it.

The Character of the Market

What Makes Draper Unique for Investors

Draper runs upmarket. Entry prices are higher than most of Salt Lake County, cap rates are tighter, and the game here is less about monthly cash flow and more about owning a high-quality asset that carries itself while appreciating. Corner Canyon and SunCrest especially — these aren’t neighborhoods where you’re going to buy a cheap duplex and BRRRR your way out. They’re neighborhoods where a well-priced single-family with mountain views rents to a tech executive who signs a two-year lease and barely contacts you.

The tenant base is the second thing to understand. Draper sits on the south side of the Point of the Mountain — the north shoulder of the same tech corridor that drives Lehi. Many of the same employers are within a 10-15 minute commute: eBay, 1-800 Contacts, the Silicon Slopes cluster. That means Draper competes for high-income renters who could live in Lehi too, and the trade-off is usually around schools, mountain access, and the feel of the neighborhood.

Short-term rental rules in Draper vary by HOA and by the city’s own ordinance, and the picture changes often. Many of the newer master-planned communities prohibit STRs through the HOA regardless of city rules. Before offering on anything you plan to run as an STR, confirm both the city’s current status and the HOA’s covenants — one "no" on either side kills the strategy.

Pick Your Play

Best Strategies for Draper

Four strategies that fit the upper-tier south-valley market — chosen for the stock, the tenant base, and the exit profiles that actually show up here.

Steady Cash Flow

Long-Term Rentals in Draper

High-end single-family and townhome rentals in Draper lease to tech-industry households who want stability. Longer tenancies, cleaner tenants, and a very low turnover rate once you find the right fit — the trade-off is a tighter cap rate at purchase.

Nightly Premium

Short-Term Rentals in Draper

Where the city and HOA both allow it, Draper STRs can pull strong nightly rates from business travelers headed to Silicon Slopes offices and leisure guests using Corner Canyon trails. Confirm rules at both layers before offering — many master-planned HOAs prohibit STRs outright.

Live-In Entry Point

House Hacking in Draper

Draper’s larger single-family homes often include a walk-out basement or a lockable in-law suite that works as a second unit. Owner-occupant financing on a higher-priced home, offset by a tenant paying mountain-view rent for the lower unit, gets a first-time investor into a premium address.

Keep More of the Return

Cost Segregation in Draper

Higher-basis Draper rentals are strong candidates for a cost segregation study, because the dollar value of the accelerated depreciation scales with the purchase price. Talk to a Utah-licensed CPA about whether the study pays for itself on your specific property.

What to Buy Here

Rental Property Types That Work in Draper

Corner Canyon luxury single-family

Larger, newer homes in Corner Canyon command top-tier Salt Lake County rents. Tenants here are often tech executives or professionals who sign longer leases and treat the property like their own. Thin margin, strong appreciation history.

SunCrest townhome

SunCrest townhomes on the ridge between Draper and Alpine offer a mountain-view address at a lower per-door price than Corner Canyon single-family. HOA fees are higher, but exterior maintenance is handled and tenant pool skews stable.

Steep Mountain Drive single-family

The Suncrest and Steep Mountain corridor sits above the valley with views and quick access to I-15 and the Point of the Mountain employers. Larger homes here rent as premium long-term product; smaller ones can work as mid-term furnished rentals.

Draper Historic Main older home

The older stock near Draper’s historic Main Street is the one pocket of Draper where value-add plays still show up — smaller homes, less perfect condition, and real room to rehab and reposition at a rent that still beats the submarket.

Mid-term furnished rental for tech executives

Thirty-plus-day furnished rentals in Draper work well for tech executives relocating, extended consulting engagements, and corporate housing tied to Point of the Mountain employers. Sidesteps most STR ordinance exposure and still prices above long-term unfurnished.
