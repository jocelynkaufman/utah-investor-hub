The Lehi Market

Real Estate Investing in Lehi

Lehi is the center of Utah’s tech economy. Adobe, Micron, Xactware, and the rest of the Point of the Mountain corridor sit here, pulling in engineers, product managers, and support staff from across the country. For investors, that one fact drives almost everything about the rental market — who the tenants are, what they’ll pay, and what kind of property actually gets leased on the first weekend of listing.

Lehi is also one of Utah’s youngest big-city markets. Most of the housing stock went up after 2000, so the strategies that work here lean toward buy-and-hold on newer product — not deep value-add — with a tenant base that can carry higher rents because of where they work.

The Character of the Market

What Makes Lehi Unique for Investors

Lehi’s rental story starts and ends with its employer base. The Silicon Slopes corridor — Adobe’s campus, Micron’s manufacturing footprint, Xactware, and dozens of smaller tech companies clustered around Thanksgiving Point and Traverse Mountain — keeps a steady inflow of high-income renters moving in from out of state. These are tenants who can afford Lehi rents, who often prefer renting while they figure out whether the job is going to stick, and who want newer, cleaner properties within a short commute of the office.

The housing stock reflects that. Lehi grew most of its footprint after 2000, which means most of what you’ll find listed is relatively modern — Thanksgiving Point townhomes, Traverse Mountain single-family, newer townhome communities along the Jordan River Parkway corridor. There’s less older stock to buy below market and rehab, so classic BRRRR is harder to make pencil here than in Salt Lake or Ogden. The money in Lehi is in buying good product and holding it against a rising rent floor.

One thing to watch: Lehi’s short-term rental rules are restrictive and have shifted in recent years. Before offering on anything with STR cash-flow in your underwriting, confirm the current rules with the city’s planning department. The long-term and mid-term furnished-rental paths are far more defensible here.

Pick Your Play

Best Strategies for Lehi

Four strategies that fit the tech-corridor economy — chosen for the stock, the tenant base, and what’s actually legal to rent here.

Steady Cash Flow

Long-Term Rentals in Lehi

A 12-month lease on a newer Lehi townhome or single-family is one of the cleanest rental plays in Utah County. Tech workers signing their first Lehi job want stability, they’ll pay above-average for a modern unit, and they turn over less often than you’d expect.

Live-In Entry Point

House Hacking in Lehi

Thanksgiving Point and Traverse Mountain townhome layouts often include a lockable basement or a separate entry suite that works for owner-occupant house hacking. Buy the whole unit with low-down-payment financing, live in one half, rent the other half to a Silicon Slopes commuter.

Multiple Units, One Closing

Multi-Family in Lehi

Small multifamily in Lehi is mostly newer townhome rows and infill fourplexes rather than classic duplex stock. Two-to-four unit buildings still qualify for residential loans, and the tenant pool is deep enough to fill them at strong per-door rents.

Keep More of the Return

Cost Segregation in Lehi

Newer Lehi rentals are ideal candidates for a cost segregation study. The construction components depreciate on faster schedules than straight-line, which can turn a positive cash-flow property into a tax-advantaged one. Talk to a Utah-licensed CPA before you commit.

What to Buy Here

Rental Property Types That Work in Lehi

Thanksgiving Point townhome

Newer-build townhomes near Thanksgiving Point and Traverse Mountain are the cleanest Lehi rental product — walkable to tech employers, modern finishes that need minimal turn work, and a tenant pool of engineers and PMs who will sign a 12-month lease at list price.

Traverse Mountain single-family

Larger single-family homes in Traverse Mountain lease to tech-industry households that want a yard and a view without leaving the commute zone. Higher rents, longer tenancies, and lower turnover than the townhome side of the market.

Jordan River Parkway townhome

Newer townhome communities along the Jordan River Parkway corridor sit between Lehi’s tech core and the I-15 commute. Strong rental demand, lower price per door than Thanksgiving Point, and a straightforward long-term-rental underwrite.

New-construction single-family

Buying new direct from a builder is a real Lehi path — incentives shift, but when they’re favorable (rate buy-downs, closing-cost credits), the per-month math on a new single-family rental can beat resale. Underwrite conservatively on rent and assume full occupancy from month two.

Mid-term furnished rental for tech executives

Thirty-plus-day furnished rentals dodge most of the STR-ordinance exposure and still command a premium over long-term unfurnished rent. Tech relocations, consulting engagements, and corporate housing contracts are the main demand drivers.
