Nightly Income in Tourism Markets

Short-Term Rentals in Utah

Airbnb, VRBO, and nightly vacation rentals in Utah's tourism-driven markets. Higher revenue potential than long-term rentals — and more hands-on management.

Work With Stodd Group

The Strategy

What Short-Term Rentals in Utah Means

Short-term rentals (STRs) are properties rented out by the night or week instead of the month. Instead of one tenant on a 12-month lease, you have dozens of guests across the year, booked through Airbnb, VRBO, or direct.

STR revenue per property can be 1.5–3x higher than a long-term lease in the right market — but so are expenses (cleaning, supplies, dynamic pricing, higher furnishing cost) and so is management intensity.

STRs also qualify for bonus depreciation under cost segregation when run as an active business (material participation rules apply). The tax treatment can be significantly better than long-term rentals.

The Utah Edge

Why It Works in Utah

Utah has world-class tourism anchors: Park City and Deer Valley (ski), Moab and Zion (national parks), St. George (retirees + golf + winter escape), Bear Lake (summer), and Logan/Cache Valley (university).

STR regulations in Utah are strictly local and vary wildly by city. Park City requires a license and has zone restrictions. Moab is tightening every year. Some SLC neighborhoods ban nightly rentals entirely. Do the zoning research before you offer on anything.

Legal, well-run STRs in approved zones in Utah can run 65–80% occupancy at nightly rates that make long-term landlords jealous — but the rule risk is real and the regulatory environment keeps moving.

Getting Started

How to Begin

1

Pick the market and verify STR is legal

Call the city planning department before you even look. Get the current rules on licensing, zoning, occupancy limits, and HOA restrictions in writing.

2

Buy a property that fits the market

Park City ski-in/ski-out is a different product than a Moab basecamp or a St. George snowbird condo. Design for the guest.

3

Furnish and launch professionally

Great photos, clear description, competitive pricing. First-30-days reviews set your rank for the next year.

4

Set up operations

Dynamic pricing (PriceLabs, Wheelhouse), cleaner rotation, restocking, maintenance response. Co-hosting or professional management is worth considering.

5

Measure and optimize

Track occupancy, ADR, and RevPAR monthly. Adjust pricing, minimum stays, and target guest profile as the data comes in.
