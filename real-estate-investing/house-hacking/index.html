Live for Less, Build Wealth Faster

House Hacking in Utah

The single best way to get into Utah real estate with the least money down. Buy a 2–4 unit property or single-family home, live in one portion, rent out the rest, and let your tenants cover most (or all) of your mortgage.

Work With Stodd Group

The Strategy

What House Hacking in Utah Means

House hacking is buying a property with owner-occupant financing (as little as 3.5% down with FHA, 5% down with conventional) and renting out part of it to offset your housing cost. The three most common shapes in Utah:

Small multifamily (2–4 units). A duplex, triplex, or fourplex where you live in one unit and rent the others. Still qualifies for low-down-payment residential financing.

Single-family with ADU or basement apartment. Many older Salt Lake and Ogden homes have mother-in-law suites. You live in the main house, rent the ADU.

Rent-by-the-room. Buy a 3-4 bedroom SFH, live in one bedroom, rent the others to roommates. Lowest barrier to entry.

The Utah Edge

Why It Works in Utah

Utah has one of the fastest-growing populations in the country, which drives persistent rental demand along the Wasatch Front. Salt Lake County, Utah County, and Davis County all have solid tenant pools and steady rent growth.

The state has deep stock of small multifamily properties in older Salt Lake, Ogden, Provo, and Logan neighborhoods — built when duplex construction was common. These are the ideal house hack vehicles.

Owner-occupant financing (FHA, VA, UHC) is available on 1–4 unit properties, so a first-time Utah buyer can get into a fourplex with a 3.5% down payment and have tenants cover the mortgage.

Getting Started

How to Begin

1

Get pre-approved for owner-occupant financing

Start with a lender who knows FHA, VA, and UHC loan programs. Find out your real budget before you look at properties.

2

Pick your target neighborhoods

Rental demand matters more than curb appeal. Focus on areas near job centers, universities, and transit along the Wasatch Front.

3

Analyze every candidate for cash flow

Rent for the non-owner units has to cover PITI plus a vacancy/maintenance reserve. Run numbers before you make any offer.

4

Move in, screen tenants carefully

Your first tenant decision sets the tone for the whole house hack. Screen credit, income, rental history, and references.

5

Repeat after 12 months

Owner-occupancy requirements usually lift at 12 months. You can buy another owner-occupant property, move in, and turn the first one into a full rental.
