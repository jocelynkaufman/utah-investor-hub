The Resort & Appreciation Markets

Real Estate Investing in Summit & Washington County

Utah’s two resort and tourism counties sit on opposite ends of the state — Summit County up in the Wasatch Back around Park City, Washington County down in the red-rock corner near St. George. Geographically they couldn’t be further apart. Thematically, they’re the same investor conversation: appreciation-driven, short-term-rental heavy, licensing-sensitive, and emphatically not a cash-flow story.

Both counties attract a different kind of investor than the Wasatch Front. Entry prices are high, cap rates are thin, and STR licensing changes year to year. The reward is appreciation that has compounded hard over the past decade plus and a demand base that doesn’t depend on local employment — it comes from out of state, every year.

The Character of These Markets

What Makes These Counties Different

Summit County runs on ski. Park City Mountain Resort and Deer Valley Resort anchor the winter economy, Sundance Film Festival anchors January, and summer trail/mountain-bike tourism fills the shoulders. Snyderville Basin — the unincorporated ring around Park City — carries much of the newer SFH and townhome stock. Heber Valley to the south (Heber City and Midway) picks up the overflow from the Park City employment base, with slightly lower entry prices and a growing year-round residential market. Across all of Summit County, nightly-rental licensing is strict, zoning varies tile by tile, and HOA rules often override city rules. Confirming licensing is the single most important due-diligence step on any offer here.

Washington County runs on winter escape and national parks. St. George is the population anchor and one of the fastest-growing cities in the Mountain West, pulling retirees, remote workers, and LDS families moving south from the Wasatch Front. Hurricane sits on the way to Zion National Park and has become a meaningful STR submarket in its own right. Washington City, Santa Clara, and Ivins fill in the corridor — family-rental and retiree-rental demand plus some shoulder STR inventory. Regulations here are also meaningfully tighter than they used to be, and Washington County cities have been adjusting ordinances almost annually.

The investor thesis in both counties is the same: appreciation and demand that comes from people who don’t live in Utah. That means the risks are also similar — regulatory risk from local STR ordinances, softer cash flow than the Wasatch Front, and a buyer pool that is sensitive to broader economic conditions. These are not first-deal markets. They’re markets for investors who already have one or two Wasatch Front properties under their belt and are looking to diversify into appreciation plus some STR upside.

Zoom In

Cities in Summit & Washington We Cover

Eight submarkets across the resort and tourism corners of Utah — ski economy in the north, red-rock economy in the south, STR-sensitive everywhere.

The Flagship Resort Market — Summit

Park City

Park City Mountain and Deer Valley anchor a luxury STR market with tight nightly-rental licensing and some of the highest entry prices in the state. Old Town condos, ski-in/ski-out homes, and Snyderville Basin townhomes make up most of the investor stock. Appreciation-first, STR-second, cash-flow a distant third.

Deep dive into Park City

Park City Overflow — Summit/Wasatch

Heber

Just south of Park City over the pass. Heber Valley has become the practical bedroom community for Park City workers and remote professionals who want mountain living without Park City pricing. Growing year-round rental demand plus some STR upside where zoning allows. A more balanced appreciation-and-cash-flow story than Park City proper.

Park City’s Suburban Ring

Snyderville Basin

The unincorporated ring around Park City (Jeremy Ranch, Pinebrook, Silver Springs, Kimball Junction). Newer townhome and SFH stock, Park City School District, and a mix of long-term rentals plus licensed STRs. Zoning and HOA STR rules vary by subdivision — every address needs its own licensing check.

The South-End Growth Engine — Washington

St. George

The largest and fastest-growing city in southern Utah. Retirees from the Wasatch Front, remote workers, and LDS families moving south have driven a decade of appreciation. Long-term rental demand is strong, STR demand is strong in licensed zones, and the construction pipeline keeps running. One of the most-asked-about markets in Utah.

On the Way to Zion — Washington

Hurricane

Sits between St. George and Zion National Park. Has become a meaningful STR submarket on the back of national-park tourism, plus a growing year-round residential base. Licensing is specific to zone and city — always verify current STR ordinances before offering.

Family Rental Corridor

Washington City

The eastern neighbor to St. George. Primarily a family-rental and owner-occupant submarket with newer SFH subdivisions, quiet long-term rental math, and a more modest STR presence. Lower entry prices than St. George proper for similar quality stock.

Established Residential

Santa Clara

A small, established city on the west side of St. George. Newer SFH, strong appreciation, well-regarded schools, and a tenant base that leans toward long-tenure families. A quieter, less-STR-driven play than Hurricane or St. George proper.

Retirement & Upscale SFH

Ivins

Tucked against Snow Canyon State Park. Older retiree-heavy neighborhoods plus newer upscale SFH subdivisions. Lower STR density than Hurricane and St. George, higher share of long-term and seasonal rentals. Appreciation-first with a consistent retiree/second-home buyer pool.

Pick Your Play

Best Investing Strategies for Resort & Tourism Markets

Four strategies that fit Utah’s appreciation-first markets — STRs where licensing allows, long-term rentals in the bedroom communities, and multi-family where the stock exists.

The Flagship Strategy Here

Short-Term Rentals in Resort Markets

The default play in Park City, St. George, and Hurricane — where licensing allows. Confirm current STR ordinances for the specific address, parcel, and HOA before writing an offer. Licensing rules in both counties have tightened over the past several years and continue to change.

Steady Cash Flow

Long-Term Rentals in Heber & Washington City

Heber, Washington City, Santa Clara, and Ivins are the quieter long-term rental submarkets inside these counties — less STR exposure, steadier tenants, and more predictable management. The right fit for investors who want geographic exposure to appreciation without taking on STR operational risk.

Learn the strategy

Buy, Renovate, Resell

Fix & Flip in St. George

Washington County’s appreciation profile plus a steady buyer pool of retirees and relocating families creates a reasonable flip environment in St. George and Hurricane. Older ramblers get renovated into modern finishes, and the exit buyer typically moves quickly. Works best for experienced operators who understand local build costs.

Learn the strategy

Multiple Units, One Closing

Multi-Family in St. George

Washington County has a growing small-multifamily inventory, plus a workforce-rental demand base that supports steady 12-month leases in units separate from the STR market. Residential financing still applies on 2–4 unit buildings. A cleaner long-term hold than most STR exposure.
