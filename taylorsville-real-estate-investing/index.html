The Taylorsville Market

Real Estate Investing in Taylorsville

Taylorsville sits right in the middle of the Salt Lake Valley — west of I-215, south of West Valley, and far enough from downtown that prices still pencil out for a working investor. It’s one of the most affordable paths into a Salt Lake County rental, and the housing stock is old enough that value-add actually shows up.

The tenant pool is deep and varied — working households, Salt Lake Community College students at the Redwood campus, Trax Red Line commuters pulling into Salt Lake for jobs. And the entry prices are a step below Draper or the east bench, which means a first-time investor can actually get a Salt Lake County deal closed here.

The Character of the Market

What Makes Taylorsville Unique for Investors

Taylorsville is the middle of the valley — literally and demographically. Most of the housing stock went up between the late 1960s and the mid-1980s, which puts the city in a pricing band that’s far more accessible than Draper to the south, Holladay to the east, or newer Lehi to the south. For investors, that older stock means something specific: dated kitchens, original baths, and the kind of cosmetic rehab math that makes BRRRR actually pencil here in a way it doesn’t in newer suburbs.

The tenant base is deep. Salt Lake Community College’s Redwood Road campus pulls a constant stream of year-round students who need affordable rentals within bus range. Trax Red Line stations put downtown Salt Lake within a 20-25 minute ride without a car, which makes Taylorsville an easy sell to tenants whose jobs are in SLC proper. The rental pool is broad, turnover is steady, and the per-door price point is forgiving.

The flip side: cap rates are compressed here too compared to Ogden or the west side of Salt Lake. Taylorsville isn’t a screaming cash-flow market — it’s a reliable one. The play is the combination of lower-entry-price-than-SLC plus real value-add room on older stock.

Pick Your Play

Best Strategies for Taylorsville

Four strategies that fit the mid-valley market — chosen for the stock, the tenant base, and the entry price point that actually gets first deals closed.

Recycle Your Capital

BRRRR in Taylorsville

1970s and 1980s ramblers with dated interiors are the Taylorsville BRRRR target. Buy below retail, rehab kitchens and baths, refinance against the new value, and pull most of your capital back out. The rental demand on the back end is deep enough that leasing isn’t a question mark.

Live-In Entry Point

House Hacking in Taylorsville

Taylorsville ramblers often have finished or finishable basements that qualify for separate ADU status. Owner-occupant financing on the whole house, live upstairs, rent the ADU to a Trax commuter or an SLCC student, and let a tenant carry most of your mortgage.

Steady Cash Flow

Long-Term Rentals in Taylorsville

A rehabbed single-family rental in Taylorsville runs through the standard SLC-metro 12-month cycle, but at a lower entry price than SLC proper. Tenants here are working households who want proximity to I-215 and Red Line Trax — stable, predictable leasing.

Multiple Units, One Closing

Multi-Family in Taylorsville

Small multifamily buildings — duplexes and older triplexes — sit along the older Redwood Road and 4700 South corridors. These still qualify for residential loans (2-4 units) and scale faster per closing than single-family while staying in the city’s accessible price band.

What to Buy Here

Rental Property Types That Work in Taylorsville

1970s rambler with legal ADU potential

The classic Taylorsville rental play. Older rambler stock with an unfinished basement or a partially finished lower floor that can be brought up to code and permitted as a conforming ADU. Two legal rents under one roof, at a Salt Lake County price that’s still reachable.

Duplex along Redwood Road or 4700 South

Small multifamily in the older corridors is where the per-door math really tightens up. Two-to-four unit buildings still qualify for residential financing, and the corridors have transit access that keeps demand strong.

Split-level or tri-level near I-215

Split-level stock from the late 70s and 80s is common in Taylorsville and lends itself to house-hacking with a lower-level rental suite. Easy commute access to I-215 and I-15 makes these broadly appealing to working tenants.

Newer townhome in a pocket development

The newer townhome communities tucked into redeveloped parcels along Bangerter and 2700 West give Taylorsville some newer product that needs less maintenance. Higher HOA fees, but the turn work between tenants is minimal.

Small single-family near SLCC Redwood campus

Smaller older homes within a short bus ride of the SLCC Redwood campus lease to year-round students who want their own address rather than dorms or shared apartments. Steady demand, and students here tend to re-sign for a second year.
