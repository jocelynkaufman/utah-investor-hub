The Tooele County Market

Real Estate Investing in Tooele County

Tooele County sits on the west side of the Oquirrh Mountains, a straight 45-minute shot down I-80 from downtown Salt Lake. For a long time it was its own economic island — mining, Tooele Army Depot, and the ranching communities of the Rush and Tooele Valleys. Over the last decade it has quietly become a Salt Lake commuter market. Housing on the west side of the mountains runs meaningfully below Salt Lake County suburb pricing, the I-80 commute keeps SLC jobs in reach, and master-planned growth in Stansbury Park and Lake Point has turned the north end of the county into a legitimate bedroom community.

For investors, this is a value SFH-rental play. Lower entry prices than Herriman or South Jordan, a tenant base that splits between SLC-commuting workers, Tooele Army Depot employees, and the mining and logistics economy, and a growth story that still has room to run as the Wasatch Front keeps pushing west.

The Character of the County

What Makes Tooele County Work for Investors

The Tooele Valley sits behind the Oquirrh Mountains on the western edge of the greater Salt Lake metro. I-80 cuts east-to-west across the top of the valley connecting it to downtown SLC in about 45 minutes outside of peak traffic, and SR-36 runs south from the interstate through Stansbury Park, Erda, Grantsville, and Tooele proper. That commuter geography is the whole reason the county has shifted from economic island to bedroom market. The housing on the Tooele Valley floor costs meaningfully less per square foot than comparable stock in Herriman, South Jordan, or West Jordan, and for a tenant who can absorb the drive, the value math is real.

The in-county economy is three layers. First, Tooele Army Depot anchors a federal-employment base the way Hill AFB does in Davis County — different scale, same demand-floor dynamic. Second, the mining and metals economy around Kennecott and the Stockton/Rush Valley area keeps a blue-collar rental pool deep. Third, the newer logistics and distribution corridor at the north end of the valley — along I-80 toward the lake — has pulled in warehouse and fulfillment-center employment that didn’t exist a decade ago. On top of all of that, master-planned growth around Stansbury Park, Lake Point, and newer Tooele subdivisions is bringing in a steady stream of first-time buyers and SFH renters who work in Salt Lake County.

The rental math leans straightforward buy-and-hold. Tenants here want three bedrooms, a yard, a garage, and a commute they can live with. Appreciation has tracked the broader Wasatch Front over the last several years and the undervalued-vs-Salt-Lake-County spread keeps the thesis interesting on a long hold. STRs exist but aren’t the play. BRRRR math works in the older pockets of Tooele proper but the renovated-comp spread is narrower than Ogden’s. Think of Tooele County as the value SFH-rental tier of the metro — the place where a straightforward long-term rental actually pencils without having to chase a BRRRR or STR thesis.

Zoom In

Cities in Tooele County We Cover

Six submarkets across the Tooele Valley — Tooele proper and Grantsville anchor the south, Stansbury Park and Lake Point carry the north-end commuter growth.

The County Seat

Tooele

The largest city in the county and the commercial and government center. Older mid-century stock around Main Street, newer SFH subdivisions on the south and east sides, and a tenant pool split between Tooele Army Depot workers, mining-economy employees, and SLC commuters. The deepest rental inventory in the county and the most straightforward entry point for a first deal.

The Ranching Town

Grantsville

West of Tooele in the open valley, Grantsville has kept more of its ranching-town character while absorbing meaningful master-planned SFH growth on its east side. Larger lots, newer family neighborhoods, and a tenant base that leans long-tenure families. Quieter management profile than Tooele proper.

The Commuter Growth Node

Stansbury Park

North of Tooele along SR-36, Stansbury Park is the north-valley master-planned community that drives a lot of the county’s growth story. Newer SFH, HOA-governed neighborhoods, young families, and a buyer pool that weighs the Salt Lake commute carefully. Appreciation-leaning with strong long-term rental demand.

Quiet Mid-Valley

Erda

Between Tooele and Stansbury Park, Erda is a lower-density mix of larger-lot SFH and some newer subdivision infill. A quieter tenant pool, a long-hold orientation, and an appreciation profile that tracks the broader north-valley growth. Limited rental inventory means fewer tenant comps than Tooele or Stansbury Park.

The I-80 Gateway

Lake Point

Right at the I-80 exit at the north end of the Tooele Valley. The closest Tooele County submarket to downtown Salt Lake by drive time, and the one that feels the commuter story most directly. Newer SFH subdivisions, strong appreciation story as the logistics and distribution corridor keeps growing along I-80.

Rural South-County

Vernon

Far south in the Rush Valley, Vernon is a small, rural community with a limited housing inventory and a very different rental profile from the north-valley commuter market. Not a core investor submarket, but worth noting as the rural edge of the county for anyone considering a land-plus-SFH long-hold.

Pick Your Play

Best Investing Strategies in Tooele County

Four strategies tuned to a value commuter market — long-term SFH rentals, house hacking in Tooele proper, selective BRRRR, and small multi-family where it shows up.

The Core Tooele Play

Long-Term SFH Rentals

Tooele, Grantsville, Stansbury Park, and Lake Point are classic SFH long-term rental territory. Tooele Army Depot families, SLC commuters, and the mining-and-logistics workforce all keep leases quiet. Lower entry prices than any Salt Lake County suburb and a tenant pool that stays deep year-round.

Live-In Entry Point

House Hacking in Tooele

The older mid-century stock in Tooele proper regularly comes with basement layouts that house-hack cleanly. Owner-occupant financing on a 2–4 unit or a legal-basement-apartment SFH is a practical first-deal path — especially for an investor who works in SLC and can absorb the I-80 commute during the owner-occupant year.

Recycle Your Capital

Selective BRRRR

The older pockets of Tooele proper and parts of Grantsville carry homes that trade below renovated comps. The BRRRR spread is narrower than Ogden’s, but exit rents are supported by Tooele Army Depot and the north-valley commuter pool. Best treated as a smaller-count, higher-care rehab strategy — not a volume BRRRR market.

Learn the strategy

Multiple Units, One Closing

Small Multi-Family

Small-multifamily inventory in Tooele County is thinner than in Ogden, but duplexes and the occasional 2–4 unit building do show up in Tooele proper and Grantsville. Workforce rental demand from Tooele Army Depot and the mining economy supports long-term leases. Residential financing still applies on 2–4 units.
